Living trees (2026-09-18): stage46 moves from `P` to `C`. The runtime drew a tapered column of Living Wood, two straight branches and two filled ellipses of leaves from invented draws, which is the "giant trees that look nothing like the original" a tester reports. `WorldGen.GrowLivingTree` is five stages that share one stream: a trunk grown upward from a randomly widened base that narrows by alternating sides at random intervals and records each narrowing as a branch anchor; a branch grown from every anchor except the last, wandering one row in ten instead of advancing and thickening every few tiles; a crown grown from the trunk top as a wobbling stem with side limbs on independent left and right cooldowns, each limb forking into a pair of anchors; roots driven down through one to five solid cells and then outward; and finally one canopy blob per recorded anchor, a circle for a crown anchor and a squashed diamond for a branch anchor. All five are now ported, together with the pass that chooses where they go.

The decisive discovery was not in `GrowLivingTree` at all. The canopy hangs Plant Detritus from its underside through `WorldGen.PlaceTile`, and `PlaceTile` ends every object placement with `SquareTileFrame`, which for the three-by-two pile family reaches `Check3x2`. `Check3x2` re-derives the object's origin from the cell's own frame and destroys the whole object when any of its six cells no longer carries the frame that origin implies, so a new object that overlaps an older one deletes the older one, through `KillTile`, which leaves frames of $-1$ rather than zero. That deletion feeds straight back into the caller: the canopy walks down a column until it meets an active cell, so a deleted cell moves where the next walk stops and therefore whether the next style draw happens at all. Without it the port matched the source's RNG on three of four cases and diverged on the fourth by a single canopy radius. The framing slice is ported for exactly that family and refuses any style whose ground requirement would need vanilla's snow, ice, mud and sand conversion sets, because a silent guess there would be worse than a loud failure.

Evidence: six official comparisons on the pinned Windows build, three seeds against both ordinary and patch mode, each checking the next shared RNG and a SHA-256 over every field of every cell in the world, plus a refusal case that must not touch a cell or draw a value. The fixture opens a cavity under the trunk base so the source's root passage, its secret room, `GrowLivingTree_HorizontalTunnel` and `AddBuriedChest` stay out of the comparison; none of those is ported. Five negative controls fail 4, 2, 6, 3 and 6 of the six cases: the framing epilogue removed, the trunk width recomputed after a patch narrows the base, the crown's limb cooldown guard inverted, the detritus style drawn before the leaf test, and the canopy scanned row-major instead of column-major. A sixth control - destroying only the framed cell instead of the whole object - fails nothing, so the object-wide destruction loop is followed from the source rather than proven by these fixtures.

Stage46 is `C`, not `E9`: the pass that selects sites is ported from the source too, including the retry loop, the ten-tile living-wood exclusion, the hundred-by-hundred dungeon-brick and cloud box and the three companion trees either side, but the source also refuses a column within fifty tiles of a recorded mountain-cave entrance (`GenVars.mCaveX`) and this runtime's Mountain Caves pass does not record those. The ledger becomes **35P + 37C = 72 unfinished rows**; **31 E9 / 279 checkpoints** is unchanged.

Pyramids (2026-09-18): the builder behind stage44 is replaced, and the row stays `P` because its chest is still open. The runtime built a solid wedge sized by two invented draws and nothing else, which is exactly the "unfinished structure with no descent" a tester reports. `WorldGen.Pyramid` is four things after the wedge: a sand-capped entry cut through one flank, a staircase that switches direction every few tens of rows as it descends, one treasure chamber opened part-way down, and a wandering exit tunnel that keeps digging until its own width breaks into open ground. All four are now ported.

Two details cost more than the rest of the method put together. The exit tunnel's column loop is written with a draw in both the initializer and the condition, and a `for` condition is re-evaluated every iteration, so the right-hand `genRand.Next(0, 2)` happens once per column rather than once per row. And the wall pass calls `SquareWallFrame` for every cell it papers, which consumes one draw per walled cell: several hundred draws per pyramid, dwarfing every other consumer in the method. Neither is visible from reading the shape of the code.

Evidence: 12 official comparisons on the pinned Windows build, each checking the next shared RNG and a hash over every cell in the world, plus a refusal case that must not touch a cell or draw a value. Six use a shallow depth that ends the descent before it can open a chamber, isolating the wedge, the interior walls, the entry cut, the staircase and the exit tunnel; six pair that with no-tunnel to isolate everything before the tunnel. The treasure chamber's own carving and decoration are ported from the same method but are NOT covered by these cases, because the source reaches them through `WorldGen.AddBuriedChest`, whose site scan and loot cascade are a separate 1,692-line primitive that is not ported. The chamber's chest is therefore filled from the runtime's buried-chest loot with the signature item the source rolled, and the shared RNG position after a pyramid still differs from the source's. Stage44 stays `P` for that reason; the geometry either side of the chest is exact and the chest is not.

No tested seed produces a pyramid at all, in either engine: Large seed 1458 has none in the official reference world and none in the runtime, and four other seeds at two sizes are likewise empty. The builder is therefore proven by the differential cases and has not been observed end to end in a generated world.

Jungle and glowing-mushroom plants (2026-09-18): stage91 moves from `P` to `C`. The runtime sampled a bounded number of random columns and produced about three percent of the source's jungle plant count, which is what made a generated jungle read as bare. The source pass is not a sampling loop at all: it is a deterministic scan of every cell from `(5, 5)` to `(maxTilesX - 5, maxTilesY - 5)`, and every active cell whose neighbour above is open gets a plant offered to it. The only probability in the jungle half is which plant. Jungle Grass is always offered; Lihzahrd Brick is offered on a one-in-five draw and only where `TooManyJunglePlantsNearby` allows it, that is at most two Jungle Plants or Jungle Plants 2 already standing in the surrounding nineteen by eleven box. Mushroom Grass below `worldSurface` is offered up to three `TryGrowingTreeByType(5, ...)` attempts, each re-checked, before falling back to a plant. A cell is never both, so the two halves interleave in the shared stream exactly as the scan visits them.

The two `WorldGen.PlaceTile` branches these need are ported as a bounded slice rather than the whole nine-hundred-line method, and any other type is refused. Both are chains of probability gates whose draws happen in order until one is taken, so the number of values consumed depends on which gate fires. For Jungle Plants a thorny bush replaces the plant on a one-in-sixteen draw below `worldSurface`, never on Lihzahrd Brick; the two wide frames at `144` and `162` additionally need the cell below `rockLayer`; the one-in-fifteen decorative branch splits again two-to-one between a two-frame and a thirteen-frame set; and everything else lands in the ordinary six-frame set. The target cell is always inactive here, so the source's `tile.Clear(Tile | TilePaint | Slope)` runs first and a generated plant never inherits paint, coating or shape. A generated thorny bush comes out at frame `0/0` because generation's `SquareTileFrame` leaves it there.

Glowing Mushroom needed `WorldGen.PlaceCatTail` and `WorldGen.GrowCatTail` as well, which are now ported. Below `worldSurface` the source offers every submerged cell to the cat tail first and only plants a mushroom when it refuses, so this is not optional decoration: without it a flooded mushroom biome gets mushrooms where the source grows cat tails, and the shared RNG diverges from that point on. The terminal growth step has three outcomes rather than one, and the draw that picks between them is short-circuited away when the clearance or water tests fail, so flowering is not simply the other two thirds.

Evidence: 16 complete-delegate comparisons of the registered pass through `GenPass.Apply` on the pinned Windows build, each a uniform full-map ground band so the whole-map scan is exercised end to end, checking the next shared RNG and a hash over every cell in the world. Four negative controls fail 2, 4, 10 and 2 of the 16 cases: one tree attempt instead of three, the wide frames ungated by `rockLayer`, the decorative frame branch inverted, and the Lihzahrd crowding rule removed. On a Large seed-1458 world the jungle plant count moves from $1\,425$ to $54\,332$ against the official server's $43\,307$; the remaining excess tracks this runtime's jungle grass surplus, which is itself at $107\%$ of the reference.

Integration consequence, fixed in the same change: growing trees in mushroom biomes exposed a gap in load-time liquid preparation, which refused any world where lava reached a tree cell because it could not resolve a tree's footprint. `WorldGen.WaterCheck` kills such a cell with a plain `KillTile(i, j)` and `KillTile` has no tree cascade, so that is a single-cell removal and the cells above simply lose their support. Tree identities are therefore admitted as single-cell loading deaths. Stage91 is `C`, not `E9`: complete-delegate equality on synthetic input, not real-prefix or whole-world equality. The ledger becomes **36P + 36C = 72 unfinished rows**; **31 E9 / 279 checkpoints** is unchanged.

Oasis (2026-09-18): stage55 moves from `P` to `C`. The runtime previously invented the whole builder - a hardcoded target of one or two basins, its own distance gates against the jungle, snow and dungeon, a `Next(22, 38)` by `Next(5, 9)` ellipse and a "seventy percent of sampled surface columns are sand" heuristic. None of that is in the source. `WorldGen.PlaceOasis` walks down from the drawn row to the first solid or walled cell, requires that cell to be Sand and to sit at least ten rows above `worldSurface`, refuses any retained basin within `350`, then draws its half-width `genRand.Next(45, 61)` and scans a box of `halfWidth + 50` by `oasisHeight + 4`. That scan has two different reaches: every solid cell in the whole box must be Sand, while liquid, walls, Sandstone and Sandstone Brick only reject inside the tighter `halfWidth` by `oasisHeight / 2` box. After acceptance it sinks the basin row until both flanks stand on walled-off solid ground, giving up after twenty rows and keeping the row it reached, which is why a cavity under one flank shifts the basin down instead of refusing the site. The basin itself is not an ellipse: the source measures a stretched radius (`|dx| * 0.7` against `|dy| * 1.35`) against a per-cell jittered threshold `halfWidth / 2 * (0.53 + NextDouble() * 0.04)`, and draws that jitter for every cell in the bounding box whether or not the cell is inside, so the rim is noisy and the RNG position depends on the box size. Outside the basin it carves an overhang above the waterline and lays Sand below it with a falloff raised to the fourth power, and a second pass bridges the surrounding shelf between solid edges up to fifty tiles apart, with a one-in-five chance of a five-to-ten-tile raised lip. Two source branches are deliberately reproduced as dead: a per-column ceiling/floor test guarded by `i > X - halfWidth / 2 && i < X - halfWidth / 2`, which no column satisfies, and a Sand-edge requirement in the shelf pass whose flag is assigned `true` immediately before it is tested. `Tile.lava(false)` is also reproduced exactly as a single-bit clear, so shimmer under the basin floor becomes honey rather than water. The retained `GenVars.oasisPosition/oasisWidth` entries are published, with the source's twenty-entry retention cap separate from placement. Evidence: 30 official `PlaceOasis` comparisons on the pinned Windows build, each checking acceptance, the retained entry, the next shared RNG and a hash over every field of every cell in the affected rectangle. Four negative controls fail 6, 2, 4 and 2 of the 30 cases: drawing the rim jitter only inside the basin, skipping the descent, making the dead per-column branch live, and resetting the liquid type to water instead of clearing one bit. Independently, a Large seed-1458 world generated by the official server contains 17 sand-floored surface pools totalling 31,445 water cells where TerraRuntime now produces 18 totalling 31,568. Stage55 is `C`, not `E9`: method equality on synthetic input plus an aggregate whole-world comparison, not real-prefix or whole-world equality. The ledger becomes **37P + 35C = 72 unfinished rows**; **31 E9 / 279 checkpoints** is unchanged.
